# MAT-2102 - Ortega v. Ridgeline Health Partners - live-demo questions

Five candidate questions for the webinar, best first. Each lists the facts a correct answer contains and the documents it
should cite. None of the figures marked "worked out" is written in any document: the assistant has to compute it.

## 1. The policy-rate calculation

**Question:** "At Ridgeline's policy rates, what would Ortega's 214 weeknights and 31 weekend days of on-call come to?
Does the $41,200 in the demand letter hold up?"

A correct answer contains:
- the rates: $95.00 per weeknight (HR-214 section 3.1) and $240.00 per weekend or holiday day (section 3.2);
- 214 x $95.00 = $20,330.00 and 31 x $240.00 = $7,440.00, total **$27,770.00** (worked out);
- so the demand letter's "$41,200.00 in on-call stipends calculated at Ridgeline's own policy rates" is wrong, by
  $13,430.00 (worked out);
- optionally: Ridgeline actually paid $26,250.00 in stipends (payroll register), so on the policy-rate measure only
  $1,520.00 is outstanding.

Cite: On-Call Compensation Policy HR-214 (3.1, 3.2); Demand letter from Reyes & Gold (section 2 and the table);
Payroll register extract (Table A).

## 2. What was actually unpaid, and why

**Question:** "What on-call pay was actually left unpaid, and why?"

A correct answer contains:
- Kronos records 214 weeknight on-call shifts; payroll paid 198; the gap of **16** is all in Q2 2025 (20 recorded,
  4 paid);
- 16 x $95.00 = **$1,520.00**;
- the cause: after the Kronos upgrade on April 3, 2025 the ONC-WN code was missing from the Lakeshore respiratory-care
  schedule template, so the 16 shifts between April 6 and June 28, 2025 generated no stipend; the code was restored on
  July 1, 2025 but the past shifts were never reprocessed;
- weekend stipends (31 days, $7,440.00) and call-back pay (126 hours) were paid in full;
- eleven other employees were affected by the same error (not part of Ortega's claim).

Cite: Timesheet summary (Total and Q2 2025 rows); Payroll register extract (note 2); HR investigation memo (3.3, 4.2-4.6).

## 3. The tolling deadline

**Question:** "When does the tolling agreement run out, and what happens if we want to end it early?"

A correct answer contains:
- the Tolling Period runs from October 6, 2026 through **January 31, 2027** (11:59 p.m. Central Time), unless extended
  in writing;
- either party may end it earlier on **fifteen (15) days' written notice**, by email plus overnight courier to the
  addresses in section 3.3 (Daniel Gold for Ortega; Andrea Lim for Ridgeline, copy to Sofia Marchetti);
- it covers only Ortega's individual wage claims, not the other employees, and is not consent to a collective or class
  action (5.1); no admission of liability (4.1); Illinois law (6.1);
- signed October 28, 2026 by Maria Ortega, Daniel Gold (Reyes & Gold) and Andrea Lim (for Ridgeline).

Follow-up worth trying: "If Ridgeline gave notice on December 1, 2026, when would tolling end?" (December 16, 2026,
worked out from section 3.2).

Cite: Tolling agreement - signed (1.2, 3.1-3.3, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, signature page).

## 4. The latest on the matter

**Question:** "What's the latest on this matter?"

A correct answer contains, in order:
- October 6, 2026: Reyes & Gold's demand for $96,764.00 (stipends $41,200.00 + overtime $7,182.00, doubled for
  liquidated damages), response requested by November 5, 2026, tolling proposed;
- October 15, 2026: Payroll's timesheet summary and payroll register (214 weeknights recorded, 198 paid);
- October 20, 2026: HR investigation memo: 16 unpaid weeknights ($1,520.00) caused by the April 3, 2025 template error;
  recommends paying $1,520.00 plus interest, paying the eleven others and auditing all schedule templates;
- October 28, 2026: tolling agreement signed, tolling through January 31, 2027;
- the file contains no response to the demand letter, so the answer should not invent one (it may note that the
  November 5, 2026 response date has passed).

Cite: Tolling agreement; HR investigation memo; Demand letter; Timesheet summary / Payroll register.

## 5. The overtime claim

**Question:** "Is the $7,182 overtime claim right?"

A correct answer contains:
- the arithmetic holds: 1.5 x $38.00 = $57.00 (offer letter), and 126 x $57.00 = $7,182.00;
- but all 126 call-back hours were paid: 88 at $57.00 ($5,016.00) for hours over 40, and 38 at the regular rate
  ($1,444.00) because they fell in weeks under 40 hours;
- the claim depends on the demand letter's theory that on-call time itself is hours worked (the "engaged to wait"
  argument), which Ridgeline disputes (free to stay home and sleep; 0.6 call-backs per on-call night); even on that
  theory the 88 hours were already paid at $57.00, so at most the overtime premium on the 38 regular-rate hours would
  be in issue.

Cite: Offer letter (pay and classification); Demand letter (section 3); Payroll register extract (Table B, note 3);
HR investigation memo (5.1, 6.1-6.2).

## Traps to avoid on stage

- Do not ask how many hours she slept, her performance ratings, her home address or union membership: none is in the
  documents (the right answer is "not stated").
- The policy number HR-214 and the 214 weeknights are a coincidence; an answer should not confuse them.

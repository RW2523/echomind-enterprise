**Re: Maria Ortega - Unpaid On-Call Wages and Overtime - Demand for Payment and Proposal to Toll**

CONFIDENTIAL SETTLEMENT COMMUNICATION - SUBJECT TO FEDERAL RULE OF EVIDENCE 408 AND ILLINOIS RULE OF EVIDENCE 408

Dear Ms. Lim:

This firm represents Maria Ortega, a Respiratory Therapist II whom Ridgeline Health Partners, Inc. ("Ridgeline") has employed at Lakeshore Medical Center since February 6, 2023. Please direct all communications concerning the matters in this letter to me.

We write to demand payment of the wages that Ridgeline has failed to pay Ms. Ortega for the time she has spent on call for its respiratory care department, and of the overtime that follows from that time, in violation of the Fair Labor Standards Act, 29 U.S.C. § 201 et seq. (the "FLSA"), and the Illinois Minimum Wage Law, 820 ILCS 105/1 et seq. (the "IMWL").

**1. Ms. Ortega's on-call time**

Ms. Ortega is a non-exempt hourly employee. Her regular rate is $38.00 per hour. In addition to her scheduled shifts, Ridgeline requires her to take on-call shifts under its On-Call Compensation Policy HR-214. Between October 1, 2023 and September 30, 2026, Ms. Ortega was on call for 214 weeknights and 31 weekend days.

The restrictions Ridgeline places on that time are severe. Under HR-214, Ms. Ortega must answer every page within ten minutes and must be able to report to Lakeshore Medical Center within forty-five minutes. Given Chicago traffic, that requirement confines her in practice to her home or its immediate vicinity for thirteen hours on each weeknight and twenty-four hours on each weekend day. She cannot travel, cannot attend events outside the immediate area, cannot drink alcohol, and cannot sleep without her pager at her side. Pages come without warning, and many require her to drive to the hospital in the middle of the night.

Time spent under those conditions does not belong to the employee. It is time during which she is "engaged to wait" for Ridgeline's benefit, and it is compensable as hours worked under the FLSA and the IMWL. See Armour & Co. v. Wantock, 323 U.S. 126 (1944); Skidmore v. Swift & Co., 323 U.S. 134 (1944); 29 C.F.R. §§ 785.15, 785.17. Illinois courts construe the IMWL consistently with the FLSA, and Illinois law provides additional remedies for its violation.

**2. Unpaid on-call wages**

Ridgeline has never paid Ms. Ortega for her on-call time as hours worked. At most it has paid a flat stipend for some of her shifts, and we expect that its own records will show that even those stipends were not paid in full.

Measured conservatively, Ms. Ortega is owed $41,200.00 in on-call stipends calculated at Ridgeline's own policy rates for the 214 weeknights and 31 weekend days on which she was on call during the period. Ms. Ortega reserves the right to claim the full value of that time at her regular rate and, where applicable, at her overtime rate, which is considerably greater.

**3. Unpaid overtime**

Because her on-call time is hours worked, Ms. Ortega's workweeks regularly exceeded forty hours. Each of the 126 hours she spent on call-backs during the period fell in such a workweek, and each should have been paid at one and one-half times her regular rate, or $57.00 per hour. Ridgeline did not pay her that overtime. Her unpaid overtime is therefore 126 call-back hours at $57.00 per hour, or $7,182.00.

**4. Liquidated damages, statutory damages and fees**

Ridgeline cannot show that it acted in good faith and with reasonable grounds for believing that its pay practices complied with the law. Under 29 U.S.C. § 216(b), Ms. Ortega is entitled to liquidated damages in an amount equal to her unpaid wages and overtime, together with her reasonable attorneys' fees and costs. Her claims under the IMWL are subject to a three-year limitations period, and the IMWL provides for treble damages, a monthly statutory penalty on each underpayment, and attorneys' fees (820 ILCS 105/12(a)). Ridgeline's practices may also violate the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act, 820 ILCS 115/1 et seq. Ms. Ortega reserves all of those claims and remedies. For the purpose of resolving this matter now, her demand is limited to liquidated damages equal to the unpaid amounts:

**5. Demand**

Ms. Ortega demands that Ridgeline pay her $96,764.00, together with her reasonable attorneys' fees and costs incurred to date, in full settlement of the claims described in this letter. Please respond in writing by November 5, 2026. If we do not receive a satisfactory response by that date, Ms. Ortega intends to file suit in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Illinois without further notice, asserting her individual claims, collective claims under 29 U.S.C. § 216(b) on behalf of similarly situated respiratory care employees, and class claims under the IMWL.

**6. Tolling**

So that the parties can explore a resolution without immediate litigation, we propose that Ridgeline and Ms. Ortega enter into a tolling agreement suspending all limitations periods applicable to Ms. Ortega's claims from the date of this letter. Every week that passes without such an agreement shortens the period for which Ms. Ortega can recover under the FLSA's two- and three-year limitations periods, and we will not allow that to happen. Please let us know promptly whether Ridgeline will agree; we will circulate a draft on request.

**7. Preservation of evidence**

Ridgeline is now on notice of Ms. Ortega's claims and must preserve all documents and electronically stored information relevant to them, including Kronos timekeeping and scheduling records, schedule templates, paging and call logs, payroll records, every version of Policy HR-214, and communications concerning on-call staffing and pay, for Ms. Ortega and for every respiratory care employee who has been on call since October 1, 2023.

This letter is not a complete statement of the facts or of Ms. Ortega's claims, and Ms. Ortega waives none of her rights. We also remind Ridgeline that the FLSA and Illinois law prohibit retaliation against employees who assert their rights to wages. Ms. Ortega remains employed at Lakeshore Medical Center, and any adverse action against her will be met with additional claims.

This firm represents Hollis Medical Supply, Inc. ("Hollis") in connection with the loss of Shipment HMS-26-0588. I write under the Temperature-Controlled Warehousing and Distribution Services Agreement between Hollis and Brennan Freight Lines, LLC ("Brennan") dated March 1, 2024, as amended by the First Amendment dated January 15, 2026 (together, the "Agreement"). Capitalized terms not defined here have the meanings given in the Agreement. Please direct all further communications on this matter to me.

**The facts**

Shipment HMS-26-0588, 22 pallets of insulin pens and vaccines, was picked up at Brennan's Eddystone facility at 6:00 a.m. on Tuesday, June 2, 2026, on trailer BFL-R-204. Under Section 7.1, Brennan was required to deliver it to Hollis's Newark DC within twenty-four (24) hours, that is, by 6:00 a.m. on June 3, 2026. At 11:20 a.m. on June 2 the trailer's reefer unit alarmed on I-95. Brennan returned the load to its yard and transferred it to trailer BFL-R-217. Brennan did not notify Hollis, as Section 6.4 requires; Hollis's receiving manager had to call Brennan's dispatch on June 3 and again on June 4 to find out where the Shipment was.

The Shipment arrived at the Newark DC on Saturday, June 6, 2026 at 9:40 a.m. The temperature at receipt was 9.8 °C. The in-load logger shows that the Goods were above 8 °C for a continuous period of 5 hours 40 minutes on June 2, with a peak of 11.4 °C, an Excursion as defined in the Agreement. Hollis quarantined the Goods. On June 10, after reviewing the manufacturers' stability guidance, Hollis's Quality Assurance department determined that none of the 22 pallets could be used. Brennan declined the opportunity to inspect them.

Brennan's Incident Report IR-26-0412, dated June 4, 2026 and sent to Hollis on July 24, states that the Shipment was delivered to the Newark DC on June 3, 2026 at 2:15 p.m. That statement is false. Hollis's contemporaneous receiving log, its quality records and its signed proof of delivery all show arrival on June 6 at 9:40 a.m. Indeed, on June 4, the date of the report, Brennan's dispatch supervisor told Hollis by telephone that the load had not yet gone out.

**Notice and claim**

Hollis gave written notice of its claim by email on June 9, 2026, within five (5) Business Days after it became aware of the Excursion on June 6, as Section 9.3 requires, and Brennan acknowledged that notice on June 11. Hollis submitted its full claim with supporting documents on July 28, 2026, well within sixty (60) days after its notice. Brennan has not paid any part of it.

Hollis requested the Logger Data for the Shipment on July 15, 2026. Section 6.3 required Brennan to provide it within two (2) Business Days. Brennan provided it on July 24, 2026, seven (7) Business Days after the request.

**Brennan's liability**

Brennan breached Section 6.1, which required it to keep the Goods at 2 °C to 8 °C at all times while they were in its custody, and Section 7.1. Under Section 10.1, Brennan is liable for Goods that cannot be used because of an Excursion, measured by their Replacement Cost plus Hollis's reasonable expedited replacement freight. Under Section 12.1 those amounts are direct damages. Under Section 7.4 Brennan must also credit Hollis $1,250.00 for each full day of delay: the Shipment was due by 6:00 a.m. on June 3 and arrived at 9:40 a.m. on June 6, three full days late.

No limitation of liability assists Brennan. Section 12.3 provides that the limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 do not apply to damages arising from Brennan's breach of Section 6. In any event, the First Amendment replaced Section 12.2, and the cap on Brennan's aggregate liability is now $1,500,000 in any Contract Year, well above Hollis's claim.

Brennan's credit memo BFL-CM-26-019 of July 20, 2026 credits $1,150.00, the freight charge for the failed Shipment. Hollis has not been charged for freight in its claim, and the credit memo does not reduce it.

Brennan is on notice of this dispute and must preserve all documents and electronically stored information relating to Shipment HMS-26-0588, including bills of lading, proofs of delivery, dispatch records and call recordings, driver logs, GPS and telematics data for the tractor and trailers used, reefer unit downloads and maintenance records for trailers BFL-R-204 and BFL-R-217, yard gate records, and all drafts of Incident Report IR-26-0412.

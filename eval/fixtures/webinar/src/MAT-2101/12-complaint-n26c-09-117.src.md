# IN THE SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF DELAWARE

HOLLIS MEDICAL SUPPLY, INC.,
Plaintiff,

v.

BRENNAN FREIGHT LINES, LLC,
Defendant.

C.A. No. N26C-09-117 CCLD

# COMPLAINT

Plaintiff Hollis Medical Supply, Inc. ("Hollis"), by and through its undersigned counsel, for its Complaint against Defendant Brennan Freight Lines, LLC ("Brennan"), alleges as follows:

## NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. This is an action for breach of a temperature-controlled warehousing and distribution contract. Brennan agreed to store and transport Hollis's insulin pens, vaccines and other temperature-sensitive medical supplies at 2 °C to 8 °C at all times and to deliver each shipment within twenty-four hours. On June 2, 2026, Brennan's refrigeration unit failed while carrying 22 pallets of Hollis's insulin pens and vaccines. Brennan did not tell Hollis. It delivered the shipment four days later, after the goods had been exposed to temperatures as high as 11.4 °C, and they had to be destroyed.

2. Hollis seeks $1,228,900.00 in damages under the express terms of the parties' contract, together with a declaration that the contract's limitation of liability does not bar or reduce Hollis's recovery, and interest and costs.

## THE PARTIES

3. Plaintiff Hollis Medical Supply, Inc. is a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business at 4400 Concord Pike, Wilmington, Delaware 19803. Hollis distributes temperature-sensitive medical supplies to hospitals and pharmacies in the Mid-Atlantic region and operates a distribution center at 300 Pencader Drive, Newark, Delaware 19702 (the "Newark DC").

4. Defendant Brennan Freight Lines, LLC is a Pennsylvania limited liability company with its principal place of business at 2200 Industrial Highway, Eddystone, Pennsylvania 19022. Brennan provides refrigerated warehousing and trucking services.

## JURISDICTION AND VENUE

5. This Court has subject matter jurisdiction over this action under 10 Del. C. § 541.

6. This Court has personal jurisdiction over Brennan because Brennan irrevocably consented to the jurisdiction of this Court in Section 18.2 of the parties' contract, and because Brennan regularly transacts business in Delaware, including by delivering goods to the Newark DC, within the meaning of 10 Del. C. § 3104(c).

7. Venue is proper in this Court. Section 18.2 of the parties' contract designates the Superior Court of the State of Delaware, Complex Commercial Litigation Division, as the exclusive forum for any action arising out of or relating to it. This action is appropriate for the Complex Commercial Litigation Division because the amount in controversy exceeds $1,000,000.

## FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

### A. The Agreement

8. Hollis and Brennan are parties to a Temperature-Controlled Warehousing and Distribution Services Agreement dated and effective March 1, 2024 (the "Original Agreement"). A true and correct copy of the Original Agreement is attached as Exhibit 1.

9. Under the Original Agreement, Brennan stores Hollis's goods at its facility in Eddystone, Pennsylvania and transports them by refrigerated truck to the Newark DC and to Hollis's customers in Delaware, Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Maryland.

10. Section 6.1 provides: "Brennan shall keep Temperature-Controlled Goods at 2 °C to 8 °C at all times while they are in its custody." Section 1.1 defines an "Excursion" as "any period of more than thirty (30) consecutive minutes during which Temperature-Controlled Goods are outside 2 °C to 8 °C."

11. Section 6.3 provides: "Brennan shall provide Logger Data within two (2) Business Days after Customer's request." Section 6.4 requires Brennan to notify Hollis promptly of any failure of refrigeration equipment affecting Hollis's goods.

12. Section 7.1 provides: "Delivery Window: each Shipment within the Territory shall be delivered within twenty-four (24) hours after its Pickup Time."

13. Section 7.4 provides: "Late Delivery Credit: for each Shipment delivered after the Delivery Window, Brennan shall credit Customer $1,250.00 for each full day (24 hours) of delay, up to a maximum of ten (10) days per Shipment."

14. Section 9.3 provides: "Customer shall give Brennan written notice of any claim for loss of or damage to Goods within five (5) Business Days after Customer becomes aware of the Excursion or damage, and shall submit its full claim with supporting documents within sixty (60) days after that notice."

15. Section 10.1 provides that Brennan is liable for loss of or damage to goods in its custody, "including Goods that cannot be used because of an Excursion," measured by "the Replacement Cost of the Goods, plus Customer's reasonable expedited replacement freight." Section 12.1 provides that Replacement Cost and expedited replacement freight "are direct damages."

16. Section 12.3 provides that the limitations of liability in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 "shall not apply to damages arising from a Party's gross negligence or willful misconduct, or from Brennan's breach of Section 6 (Temperature Control)."

17. Section 18 provides that the agreement is governed by Delaware law, designates this Court as the exclusive forum, and waives trial by jury.

### B. The First Amendment

18. On January 15, 2026, Hollis and Brennan entered into a First Amendment to the Original Agreement (the "First Amendment," and together with the Original Agreement, the "Agreement"). A true and correct copy of the First Amendment is attached as Exhibit 2.

19. The First Amendment deleted Section 12.2 of the Original Agreement in its entirety and replaced it with the following: "Brennan's aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall not exceed One Million Five Hundred Thousand Dollars ($1,500,000) in any Contract Year." The First Amendment expressly left Section 12.3 unchanged.

### C. Shipment HMS-26-0588

20. At 6:00 a.m. on Tuesday, June 2, 2026, Brennan picked up Shipment HMS-26-0588 (the "Shipment") at its Eddystone facility on trailer BFL-R-204. The Shipment consisted of 22 pallets of insulin pens and vaccines, all of them Temperature-Controlled Goods. It was a standard shipment, not a Priority Shipment.

21. Under Section 7.1, Brennan was required to deliver the Shipment to the Newark DC by 6:00 a.m. on June 3, 2026.

22. At approximately 11:20 a.m. on June 2, 2026, the refrigeration unit on trailer BFL-R-204 alarmed while the trailer was on I-95. Brennan's driver returned the load to Brennan's Eddystone yard, where it was transferred to trailer BFL-R-217.

23. Brennan did not notify Hollis of the equipment failure. On June 3 and again on June 4, 2026, Hollis's Receiving Manager called Brennan's dispatch office to ask where the Shipment was. On June 4, Brennan's dispatch supervisor told her that there was no arrival time for the Shipment.

24. The Shipment arrived at the Newark DC on Saturday, June 6, 2026 at 9:40 a.m., on trailer BFL-R-217. Hollis's receiving staff recorded the arrival in Hollis's receiving log and on the proof of delivery at the time.

25. Brennan's own incident report, dated June 4, 2026, states that the Shipment was delivered to the Newark DC on June 3, 2026 at 2:15 p.m. That statement is false. No Brennan truck delivered the Shipment to the Newark DC on June 3, 2026.

### D. The Excursion and the Loss of the Goods

26. The temperature of the Goods at receipt on June 6, 2026 was 9.8 °C. The data logger that travelled with the Shipment recorded that the Goods were above 8 °C for a continuous period of 5 hours 40 minutes on June 2, 2026, with a peak of 11.4 °C. That was an Excursion as defined in the Agreement.

27. Hollis quarantined all 22 pallets on arrival. On June 10, 2026, after reviewing the manufacturers' stability guidance, Hollis's Director of Quality Assurance determined that none of the Goods could be released for distribution to hospitals and pharmacies and that all 22 pallets had to be destroyed.

28. Hollis's documented cost to replace the Goods was $1,186,400.00. Hollis reasonably incurred $38,750.00 in expedited freight to obtain replacement product for its customers.

### E. Hollis's Notice and Claim

29. Hollis became aware of the Excursion on June 6, 2026. On June 9, 2026, within five Business Days, Hollis's General Counsel gave Brennan written notice of Hollis's claim by email, followed by a confirmation copy by overnight courier, as Sections 9.3 and 19 require. Brennan's Chief Operating Officer acknowledged the notice in writing on June 11, 2026 and stated that Brennan did not need to inspect the Goods.

30. On July 15, 2026, Hollis requested the Logger Data for the Shipment. Brennan did not provide it until July 24, 2026, seven Business Days after the request, in breach of Section 6.3.

31. On July 20, 2026, Brennan issued credit memo BFL-CM-26-019, crediting Hollis $1,150.00, the freight charge for the Shipment. Brennan has paid nothing toward the loss of the Goods and has not given the Late Delivery Credit.

32. On July 28, 2026, within sixty days after its notice, Hollis submitted its full claim with supporting documents.

### F. Brennan's Refusal to Pay

33. On August 18, 2026, Hollis, through counsel, demanded payment of $1,228,900.00 within thirty days.

34. By letter of September 2, 2026, Brennan's counsel refused. The letter asserted that the Shipment was delivered on June 3, 2026; that Section 9.3 required notice "within forty-eight (48) hours of delivery"; and that Section 12.2 caps Brennan's liability at "Five Hundred Thousand Dollars ($500,000) in any Contract Year." Each assertion is wrong. Section 9.3 contains no forty-eight hour requirement, and the language quoted as Section 12.2 is the original text that the First Amendment deleted and replaced. Brennan offered $150,000.

35. All conditions precedent to Hollis's right to recover have been performed, have occurred or have been waived.

## COUNT I
## (Breach of Contract - Temperature Control and Liability for Goods)

36. Hollis repeats and realleges paragraphs 1 through 35 as if fully set forth here.

37. The Agreement is a valid and binding contract between Hollis and Brennan. Hollis has performed its obligations under it.

38. Brennan breached Section 6.1 by failing to keep the Goods at 2 °C to 8 °C while they were in its custody; Section 6.4 by failing to notify Hollis of the failure of its refrigeration equipment; and Section 6.3 by failing to provide the Logger Data within two Business Days after Hollis's request.

39. Under Section 10.1, Brennan is liable for the Goods that could not be used because of the Excursion, measured by their Replacement Cost of $1,186,400.00 plus Hollis's reasonable expedited replacement freight of $38,750.00.

40. Because Hollis's damages arise from Brennan's breach of Section 6, Section 12.3 provides that no limitation of liability in Section 12 applies to them.

41. As a direct result of Brennan's breaches, Hollis has been damaged in the amount of $1,225,150.00, plus interest.

## COUNT II
## (Breach of Contract - Late Delivery Credit)

42. Hollis repeats and realleges paragraphs 1 through 41 as if fully set forth here.

43. Brennan breached Section 7.1 by failing to deliver the Shipment within twenty-four hours after its Pickup Time. The Shipment was due by 6:00 a.m. on June 3, 2026 and was delivered at 9:40 a.m. on June 6, 2026, three full days after the end of the Delivery Window.

44. Under Section 7.4, Brennan was required to credit Hollis $1,250.00 for each full day of delay, a total of $3,750.00. Brennan has not done so.

45. Hollis has been damaged in the amount of $3,750.00, plus interest.

## COUNT III
## (Declaratory Judgment, 10 Del. C. § 6501 et seq.)

46. Hollis repeats and realleges paragraphs 1 through 45 as if fully set forth here.

47. An actual and justiciable controversy exists between Hollis and Brennan concerning the timeliness of Hollis's notice under Section 9.3 and the application of Section 12 to Hollis's claim. Brennan contends that Hollis's notice was late and that its liability is capped at $500,000. The controversy is ripe, and a declaration will resolve it.

48. Hollis is entitled to a declaration that (a) Hollis's notice of June 9, 2026 was timely under Section 9.3; (b) Section 12.2 of the Agreement, as amended by the First Amendment, limits Brennan's aggregate liability to $1,500,000 in any Contract Year, not $500,000; and (c) under Section 12.3, neither Section 12.1 nor Section 12.2 applies to damages arising from Brennan's breach of Section 6.

## PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Plaintiff Hollis Medical Supply, Inc. respectfully requests that the Court enter judgment in its favor and against Defendant Brennan Freight Lines, LLC:

(a) on Count I, awarding Hollis damages of $1,225,150.00;

(b) on Count II, awarding Hollis damages of $3,750.00;

(c) on Count III, entering the declarations set out in paragraph 48;

(d) awarding Hollis pre-judgment and post-judgment interest at the legal rate under 6 Del. C. § 2301;

(e) awarding Hollis its costs; and

(f) granting such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

Pursuant to Section 18.3 of the Agreement, the parties have waived trial by jury.

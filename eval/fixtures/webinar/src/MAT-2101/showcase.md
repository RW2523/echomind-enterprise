## 4. "Brennan's lawyers say liability is capped at $500K and our notice was late. Are they right?"

A correct answer contains:
- **Cap:** no. Ostrow Kaplan quotes the **original** Section 12.2. The First Amendment (January 15, 2026) deleted it and replaced it with **$1,500,000 in any Contract Year**, for claims arising on or after January 15, 2026. More importantly, under **Section 12.3**, neither cap applies to damages from **Brennan's breach of Section 6 (Temperature Control)**, and Amendment Section 2.3 says so expressly. The $1,228,900.00 claim is under $1.5M anyway.
- **Notice:** no. Section 9.3 gives **five (5) Business Days after Hollis becomes aware** of the Excursion. Hollis became aware on June 6 (a Saturday), so the deadline was Friday, June 12. The June 9 email was timely, and Halvorsen acknowledged it on June 11. The "48 hours of delivery" wording appears nowhere in the Agreement, and it relies on the false June 3 date.

Cite: Ostrow Kaplan letter (September 2, 2026); First Amendment to the Warehousing and Distribution Services Agreement (January 15, 2026); Warehousing and Distribution Services Agreement - Hollis / Brennan (executed, March 1, 2024), Sections 9.3, 12.2 and 12.3; the email chain (June 9 notice, June 11 acknowledgment).

## 5. "What late-delivery credit is owed on HMS-26-0588?"

A correct answer contains: **$3,750.00**. The Shipment was picked up June 2 at 6:00 a.m. and was a standard Shipment (not Priority), so under **Section 7.1** it was due within 24 hours, by **6:00 a.m. on June 3**. It arrived **June 6 at 9:40 a.m.**, which is 3 days, 3 hours and 40 minutes late, so **3 full days × $1,250.00** (**Section 7.4**, capped at 10 days). Under Section 7.5 the credit is in addition to the Section 10 damages. The $1,150.00 credit memo is a freight reversal, not this credit.

Must not: $5,000 (four days counted from pickup) or $0 (Brennan's June 3 version).

Cite: the Agreement (Sections 7.1, 7.4, 7.5); the receiving log; the incident report (pickup time); the credit memo.

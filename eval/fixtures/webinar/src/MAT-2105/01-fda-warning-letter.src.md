Dear Mr. Lindqvist:

The United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) inspected your ready-to-eat bakery snack manufacturing facility, located at 1450 Orchard Valley Road, Wooster, OH 44691, from September 14 through September 18, 2026. During the inspection, our investigators found significant violations of the Current Good Manufacturing Practice, Hazard Analysis, and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Human Food regulation (the CGMP & PC rule) in Title 21, Code of Federal Regulations, Part 117. FDA also collected samples of your finished product during the inspection. FDA laboratory analysis of those samples found sesame, a major food allergen, that is not declared on the product label.

Based on these findings, FDA has determined that your Harvest Grain Crackers - Rosemary & Sea Salt, 7 oz (UPC 0 41620 77310 4), are misbranded within the meaning of section 403(w) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (the Act) [21 U.S.C. 343(w)]. FDA has further determined that the significant violations of the CGMP & PC rule described below cause the food manufactured at your facility to be adulterated within the meaning of section 402(a)(4) of the Act [21 U.S.C. 342(a)(4)], in that it has been prepared, packed, or held under insanitary conditions whereby it may have been rendered injurious to health.

At the close of the inspection on September 18, 2026, our investigators issued a Form FDA 483, Inspectional Observations, to you. This letter describes the most significant of the conditions they observed.

## Misbranding Violation

Your Harvest Grain Crackers - Rosemary & Sea Salt, 7 oz, are misbranded within the meaning of section 403(w) of the Act in that the finished product contains sesame, a major food allergen, and the label does not declare it. Sesame has been a major food allergen under section 201(qq) of the Act [21 U.S.C. 321(qq)] since January 1, 2023, when the amendment made by the Food Allergy Safety, Treatment, Education, and Research (FASTER) Act of 2021 took effect. A food that contains a major food allergen must declare the food source of the allergen on its label, either within the ingredient list or in a "Contains" statement immediately after or adjacent to it.

During the inspection, FDA collected samples of finished Harvest Grain Crackers - Rosemary & Sea Salt, 7 oz, from lots HG2608B and HG2609A. FDA laboratory analysis found sesame in both samples: 14 ppm in the sample from lot HG2608B and 38 ppm in the sample from lot HG2609A. The product label declares "Contains: Wheat, Soy." Sesame does not appear in the ingredient list or in the "Contains" statement.

Undeclared major food allergens present a serious risk to consumers who are allergic to them, because those consumers rely on the label to avoid the foods that can harm them. Even small amounts of an allergen can cause a severe reaction in some individuals.

## Violations of the CGMP & PC Rule

1. You did not implement your food allergen controls in a manner that protects food from allergen cross-contact. Your food safety plan identifies allergen cross-contact as a hazard requiring a preventive control for Harvest Grain Crackers produced on Line 3, and it relies on a sanitation control, your allergen changeover cleaning procedure, to control that hazard. Line 3 is shared with a seeded cracker product that contains sesame as an ingredient. Our investigators found that:

(a) your sanitation records did not document, on several occasions between July and September 2026, that the allergen changeover cleaning was performed when Line 3 changed over from the sesame-containing product to Harvest Grain Crackers, and production of Harvest Grain Crackers went ahead on those occasions; and

(b) you did not verify that the allergen changeover cleaning was performed and was effective. Your procedure relied on a visual check and sign-off, and you had no records of any verification activity, such as allergen-specific swabbing or other environmental testing, for changeovers on Line 3.

The presence of sesame in both lots sampled during the inspection shows that your sanitation controls were not adequate to prevent allergen cross-contact.

2. You did not take appropriate corrective actions when your sanitation controls were not properly implemented. When the allergen changeover cleaning on Line 3 was not performed or was not documented, you did not identify and evaluate the food produced afterwards for safety, and you did not prevent that food from entering commerce. You also did not reanalyze your food safety plan to determine whether changes were needed to reduce the likelihood that the problem would recur.

3. You did not conduct adequate verification of your preventive controls. Your sanitation and changeover records for Line 3 were not reviewed by, or under the oversight of, a preventive controls qualified individual in a manner that would have identified the missing entries and ensured that appropriate decisions about corrective actions were made.

## Additional Comments

The violations cited in this letter are not intended to be an all-inclusive list of violations at your facility or in connection with your products. You are responsible for investigating and determining the causes of any violations and for preventing their recurrence or the occurrence of other violations. It is your responsibility to ensure that your firm complies with all requirements of federal law, including FDA regulations.

You should take prompt action to address the violations cited in this letter. Failure to promptly correct these violations may result in regulatory action without further notice, including, without limitation, seizure and injunction. You should also consider what action is needed for product already in distribution, including the lots identified above and any other lots that may have been affected by the cross-contact described in this letter.

Please note that section 743 of the Act [21 U.S.C. 379j-31] authorizes FDA to assess and collect fees to cover FDA's costs for certain activities, including reinspection-related costs. Reinspection-related costs are the costs of any inspection FDA conducts after this one to determine whether your firm has achieved compliance.

Please respond in writing within fifteen (15) working days of receipt of this letter. Your response should describe the specific steps you have taken to correct the violations and to prevent their recurrence, and it should include documentation showing that the corrections have been made, such as revised labeling, revised procedures, training records and verification records. If you cannot complete all corrections within fifteen (15) working days, state the reason for the delay and the time within which you will complete the corrections.

Please include the reference CMS # 712044 in your response and send it by electronic mail to OHAFOEast.Compliance@fda.example, to the attention of the Compliance Branch, Office of Human and Animal Food Operations – East. If you have questions about this letter, you may contact the Compliance Branch at the same address.

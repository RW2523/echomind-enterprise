# MAT-2105 — Meadowgate Foods — live-demo questions

Four candidate questions for the webinar, in the order we would ask them. Each shows the facts a correct answer contains
and the documents it should cite. Titles are the imported titles (manifest). The warning letter and the label proof are
scans: the answer comes from OCR text, which is worth pointing out.

## 1. "How long do we have to respond and by when?"

A correct answer contains:
- the letter asks for a written response **within fifteen (15) working days of receipt**;
- Meadowgate **received** the letter on **November 10, 2026** (RECEIVED stamp on the scanned copy);
- the team's date is **December 1, 2026**, counted conservatively without excluding the Veterans Day and Thanksgiving
  holidays;
- if any correction cannot be finished in time, the response must give the reason and the date by which it will be done.

Cite: FDA warning letter CMS # 712044 (November 6, 2026); QA memo - sesame cross-contact on Line 3 (November 12, 2026);
optionally Draft advice memo - response to the FDA warning letter (November 14, 2026), section 3.

Do not accept: a date other than December 1, 2026, or "15 days" without "working".

## 2. "What did FDA find and why?"

A correct answer contains:
- FDA's samples from lots **HG2608B** and **HG2609A** contained **sesame** at **14 ppm** and **38 ppm**;
- the label declares only **"Contains: Wheat, Soy"**, and sesame is a major food allergen, so the crackers are
  **misbranded under section 403(w)** of the FD&C Act;
- the allergen **preventive controls** failed: **Line 3 is shared** with a sesame product and the **changeover cleaning
  was not verified** (with no corrective action and inadequate record review). FDA calls these significant violations;
- the letter warns of **regulatory action without further notice, including seizure and injunction**.

Cite: FDA warning letter CMS # 712044 (November 6, 2026); optionally Current label proof - Harvest Grain Crackers,
Rosemary & Sea Salt (the CONTAINS line).

## 3. "What are the possible sources of the sesame?"

A correct answer contains two sources:
- **main source: cross-contact on Line 3** from **Everything Seed Crisps**, because the validated changeover clean
  **SSOP-L3-07** was **skipped on July 22, August 5 and August 19, 2026** (staffing shortage on second shift);
- **possible second source: the Prairie Mills RS-440 rosemary seasoning blend**. Its certificate says the blend contains
  no sesame but is **made in a facility that also processes sesame**. The blend is being tested, partly because HG2609A
  (38 ppm) was made after a changeover that was signed off.

Cite: QA memo - sesame cross-contact on Line 3 (November 12, 2026); Supplier allergen certificate - Prairie Mills RS-440
seasoning (March 3, 2026); optionally the warning letter (shared line, unverified cleaning).

## 4. "Summarize this matter"

A correct answer contains:
- we advise **Meadowgate Foods, Inc.** on FDA warning letter **CMS # 712044** (November 6, 2026, received November 10),
  which followed the **September 14–18, 2026** inspection of the Wooster plant;
- the finding: undeclared **sesame** in Harvest Grain Crackers, Rosemary & Sea Salt, 7 oz (14 and 38 ppm); the
  **403(w)** misbranding charge; the preventive-controls failures;
- affected product: **9 lots, HG2607A–HG2609C, about 41,800 cases, six states** (OH, PA, MI, IN, KY, WV); no illness
  complaints as of November 12;
- corrective actions: Line 3 dedicated to non-sesame products from November 16; label revised to "Contains: Wheat, Soy,
  Sesame" (print order November 11); retraining; ATP and allergen swab verification on every changeover;
- our draft advice: a **voluntary recall of the 9 lots at retail level** coordinated with FDA (**expected** Class II,
  counsel's expectation, not a certainty); a **written response by December 1, 2026**; **RS-440 testing**; a **supplier
  verification update**;
- open points for the client: confirm the case count by lot; whether any product went to food service; press release
  wording.

Cite: all five documents, at least the warning letter, the QA memo and the draft advice memo.

Watch for: stating Class II as certain, naming retailers, or mentioning prior inspections, a retail price or revenue
(none of these is in the documents).

Internal note of call with operations (DEMO)  
14 August 2026  

On 14 August 2026, I, Lena Brandt, trainee at Northwind Logistics Ltd, had a call with Priya Nandakumar from the Operations department regarding the recent delivery failures and Calder Freight Ltd’s response to Northwind’s claim. The call focused on the delivery performance in April, May, and June 2026, as well as the reasons for the failures and Calder’s defense.  

The call began with a review of the on-time delivery percentages for the three months in question. April 2026 recorded a 91.5% on-time delivery rate, May 2026 was 93%, and June 2026 was 95%. Northwind’s Logistics Services Agreement with Calder Freight Ltd, signed on 3 February 2025, stipulates a 98% KPI for on-time delivery per calendar month. The service credits are calculated at 5% of the monthly fee for each full percentage point below the KPI, with a cap of 25% of the monthly fee. The monthly fee is GBP 140,000, meaning the maximum service credit available per month is GBP 35,000.  

Northwind calculated the service credits for April and May based on the shortfall from the KPI. For April, the shortfall was 6.5 percentage points, resulting in a service credit of GBP 45,500 (6.5 × 5% × GBP 140,000). For May, the shortfall was 5 percentage points, resulting in a service credit of GBP 35,000. Northwind also included June’s shortfall of 3 percentage points, which would amount to GBP 21,000, but the total service credit claim was GBP 112,000.  

During the call, Priya confirmed that the delivery failures were primarily due to issues with Calder’s subcontractors. Specifically, in May 2026, Calder’s subcontractor experienced a shortage of drivers, which directly impacted the on-time delivery performance. This issue was not covered under the force majeure clause, as the agreement explicitly excludes congestion and labour shortages at the Carrier’s subcontractors.  

Calder Freight Ltd responded to Northwind’s claim on 12 August 2026, citing force majeure related to port congestion at Felixstowe. However, the force majeure clause in the agreement only covers port closure, not congestion or subcontractor-related issues. Additionally, Calder argued that the loss-of-profit exclusion in Clause 11.4 barred Northwind from claiming for lost sales.  

Northwind’s notice of claim was sent on 28 July 2026, which is within the 30-day notice period specified in Clause 12.1. However, the notice was late for April and May, as the failures occurred in those months and the notice was not submitted until after the 30-day window. The notice was timely for June, as the failure occurred in June and the notice was submitted within 30 days.  

Priya also mentioned that the subcontractor issue in May was not disclosed to Northwind in a timely manner. Calder’s failure to inform Northwind of the subcontractor’s driver shortage may have affected the ability to mitigate the impact of the delay.  

The call concluded with an agreement to review the contractual obligations and the specific language of the force majeure and service credit clauses. It was noted that the total liability cap under Clause 11.2 is GBP 400,000 per contract year, which may limit the amount of liability Calder could be held to. However, the loss-of-profit exclusion in Clause 11.4 would prevent Northwind from recovering the GBP 260,000 in lost sales.  

In summary, the call on 14 August 2026 confirmed that the delivery failures in April and May were due to subcontractor issues, which were not covered under the force majeure clause. The notice of claim was timely for June but late for April and May. The service credit calculation is valid, but the lost sales claim is excluded under the agreement. The next step is to assess the legal implications of the late notice and the applicability of the force majeure clause.

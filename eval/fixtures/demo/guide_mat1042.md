# MAT-1042 Morgan v. Apex Industries: presenter guide

**The story.** Morgan Company LLC (the Buyer) ordered 40 IC-450 chiller units from Apex Industries, Inc. (the Supplier) for USD 1,240,000 under a Services Agreement dated April 1, 2026 (Delaware law). Amendment No. 1 (May 14, 2026) moved the Delivery Date from May 15 to May 20, 2026 and raised liquidated damages (LDs) from 1% to 1.5% of the price per week. All 40 units arrived on May 15, 2026. Two were rejected, and their replacements arrived on June 10, 2026. Morgan claims USD 55,800 in LDs. Apex says delivery was on time and relies on force majeure.

## The deliberate issues (there are no others)

1. **Arrival date.** Witness A recalls May 12. The shipment log (gate-in 07:40) and Witness B say May 15. *Show:* both dates side by side with their sources and no silent choice. A good answer adds why it matters: the inspection period is 3 Business Days (clause 6.1), and the rejection was sent on May 18.
2. **Amendment.** Clause 4.2 was May 15. Amendment No. 1, clause 1.1, makes it May 20, 2026 (clause 2.1 also changes the LD rate to 1.5%). *Show:* the amendment linked to the clause it replaces, both documents cited, and the amended values used in any calculation.
3. **Wrong figure in the draft notes.** The notes say the LD cap is "15% of the contract price (about USD 186,000)". The executed agreement says 10% (USD 124,000) in clause 7.2. *Show:* the inconsistency, with the executed agreement preferred over the draft.
4. **Unverified citation.** The memo cites *Larkspur Hydraulics Corp. v. Vantrell Fabrication Co.*, 284 A.3d 1176 (Del. 2022). The case is invented. *Show:* the citation checker lists it as the only unverified authority. The memo's other references resolve and its four quotations of the agreement are accurate.
5. **Force majeure notice.** Apex treats Priya Raman's June 2 email to Owen Tremblay as its notice. Clause 8.2 requires written notice within 5 Business Days (the flood was May 21, so the deadline was May 29), sent to the clause 14.1 address (clause 14.3) and saying that it is a clause 8.2 notice. Clause 8.3 gives no relief before such a notice. *Show:* Apex's position set against the clause, without deciding it.

The parties also disagree on two points: when Delivery was complete (May 15 or June 10) and when the invoice fell due (June 17 or July 10). These are positions in the dispute, not errors in the documents.

## Six questions for the demo

1. **"Which delivery deadline applies?"** May 20, 2026, under Amendment No. 1, clause 1.1. It replaced clause 4.2's May 15, 2026 and has been effective since May 14, 2026, when both parties signed it.
2. **"When did the units arrive at Morgan's site?"** May 15, 2026 at 07:40 according to the shipment log and Witness B. Witness A recalls May 12, 2026. The answer shows this as a conflict.
3. **"How much can Morgan claim in liquidated damages? Show the working."** USD 55,800. The delay runs from May 21 to June 10, 2026: 21 days, or 3 weeks. Each week costs 1.5% x USD 1,240,000 = USD 18,600, and 3 x USD 18,600 = USD 55,800. The cap of USD 124,000 is not reached.
4. **"What is the cap on liquidated damages?"** 10% of the Total Contract Price, USD 124,000 (clause 7.2, unchanged by the amendment). The answer flags the draft notes' 15% as inconsistent.
5. **"Can Apex rely on force majeure?"** The answer sets Apex's June 22 position (the June 2 email was notice) against clauses 8.2, 8.3 and 14.3: 5 Business Days, the notice address and the required content. It does not decide the point.
6. **"Check the citations in the research memo."** There is one external authority, *Larkspur Hydraulics*, and it is unverified. All clause references resolve to the Services Agreement, and the quotations are accurate.

I. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

A. Which Delivery Date applies to the IC-450 chiller units, and when was Delivery complete?
B. What liquidated damages can Morgan Company LLC (Morgan) claim from Apex Industries, Inc. (Apex), and is the clause enforceable?
C. Can Apex rely on force majeure to avoid liquidated damages?

II. SHORT ANSWERS

A. The Delivery Date is May 20, 2026. Amendment No. 1, dated May 14, 2026 and signed by both parties, replaced the original date of May 15, 2026 in clause 4.2 of the Services Agreement (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 1.1). Delivery was complete on June 10, 2026, when the two conforming replacement Units arrived.

B. Morgan's claim of USD 55,800 is correctly calculated: twenty-one days (three weeks) of delay at 1.5% of the Total Contract Price per week (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 2.1). It is well below the cap of 10% of the Total Contract Price, or USD 124,000, in clause 7.2 of the Services Agreement. The clause is likely to be enforced.

C. Probably not. Apex did not give a notice that complies with clause 8.2 of the Services Agreement, and clause 8.3 of the Services Agreement allows no relief for any period before a compliant notice is given.

III. FACTS

The Services Agreement is dated April 1, 2026 and covers forty (40) Units at USD 31,000 each, a Total Contract Price of USD 1,240,000 (clause 3.3 of the Services Agreement). After Apex reported a delay at its compressor supplier, Corvane Compressor Works, the parties signed Amendment No. 1 on May 14, 2026, effective that day (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 3.1). The Units left Apex's plant on May 10, 2026 and arrived on May 15, 2026. Morgan rejected Units IC450-1113 and IC450-1134 on May 18, 2026, and the replacement Units arrived on June 10, 2026. Morgan claimed USD 55,800 on June 15, 2026. Apex rejected the claim on June 22, 2026, relying on the May 15 arrival and on force majeure.

IV. ANALYSIS

A. The Delivery Date

Under clause 15.2 of the Services Agreement, an amendment must be in writing, refer to the agreement and be signed by an authorized signatory of each party. Amendment No. 1 meets each requirement. It replaces clause 4.2 of the Services Agreement so that all Units had to be delivered on or before May 20, 2026 (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 1.1), and it provides that all other terms remain in force (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 3.2).

B. When Delivery was complete

Under clause 4.4 of the Services Agreement, "Delivery is complete only when all forty (40) Units have been delivered to the Delivery Point."

The same clause 4.4 of the Services Agreement continues: "A Unit rejected under clause 6.2 is treated as not delivered until a conforming replacement Unit has been delivered to the Delivery Point."

Apex says that Delivery took place on May 15, 2026, when all forty Units arrived. That position does not fit the text of clause 4.4 of the Services Agreement. Two Units were rejected by the notice of May 18, 2026, and Delivery was complete only when their replacements arrived on June 10, 2026.

There is a risk in our own evidence. The shipment and receiving log and Simone Achterberg (Witness B) both give May 15, 2026 as the arrival date. Gareth Pell (Witness A) recalls May 12, 2026, although he accepts that he may be mistaken. The difference matters. Under clause 6.1 of the Services Agreement, the Inspection Period is three Business Days after the day of arrival. On a May 15 arrival the period ran from May 18 to May 20, 2026, and the rejection notice of May 18 was in time. On a May 12 arrival the period ended on May 15, 2026, the notice would have been late, and under clause 6.4 of the Services Agreement the two Units would have been Accepted at the end of the Inspection Period. Clause 4.4 of the Services Agreement would then not help Morgan, and its remedy for the two Units would lie in warranty under clause 9.1 of the Services Agreement rather than in liquidated damages. The documentary record supports May 15. Mr. Pell's photographs of crate 7 have not yet been recovered; their metadata may settle the point.

C. Calculation

As replaced by Amendment No. 1, ¶ 2.1, clause 7.1 of the Services Agreement provides for liquidated damages of 1.5% of the Total Contract Price for each week of delay, from the day after the Delivery Date until the day on which Delivery is complete, pro rata for part weeks.

- Delivery Date: May 20, 2026 (Amendment No. 1, ¶ 1.1)
- Delivery complete: June 10, 2026
- Period of delay: May 21, 2026 to June 10, 2026 inclusive, which is 21 days, or exactly 3 weeks
- Weekly amount: 1.5% x USD 1,240,000 = USD 18,600
- Liquidated damages: 3 x USD 18,600 = USD 55,800
- Cap under clause 7.2 of the Services Agreement: 10% x USD 1,240,000 = USD 124,000, unchanged by Amendment No. 1, ¶ 2.2

The claim also counts towards the overall limit of USD 620,000 in clause 10.2 of the Services Agreement, which it does not approach.

D. Enforceability

The governing law is Delaware law (clause 13.1 of the Services Agreement). Delaware courts will generally enforce a liquidated damages clause where the damages were difficult to estimate when the contract was made and the agreed amount is a reasonable forecast of the probable loss rather than a penalty; see Larkspur Hydraulics Corp. v. Vantrell Fabrication Co., 284 A.3d 1176, 1183 (Del. 2022) (upholding a weekly delay charge in an industrial equipment supply contract).

In clause 7.3 of the Services Agreement the parties record their agreement that the amounts are "a reasonable pre-estimate of those losses and not a penalty".

Apex may argue that the rate is charged on the whole Total Contract Price although only two Units were outstanding. Against that, the rate was negotiated in exchange for the extra time that Apex itself asked for.

E. Force majeure

Under clause 8.2 of the Services Agreement, the Affected Party may claim relief only if it gives written notice, in accordance with clause 14, within five Business Days after it first becomes aware of the Force Majeure Event. The notice must state that it is given under clause 8.2 and must describe the event, the obligations affected, its expected duration and the steps taken to mitigate it.

Notices under clause 8 of the Services Agreement are valid only if sent to the addresses in clause 14.1 (clause 14.3 of the Services Agreement), and clause 8.3 of the Services Agreement provides that "No relief is available for any period before a notice that complies with clause 8.2 is given."

Corvane's plant flooded on May 21, 2026, and Apex knew of it the same afternoon. The five Business Day period therefore ended on May 29, 2026 (May 25, 2026 was Memorial Day, a public holiday in Delaware). The email of June 2, 2026 was late. It was sent to Owen Tremblay as Project Contact rather than to Morgan's notice address, and it did not say that it was given under clause 8.2 of the Services Agreement or identify the obligations affected. Under clause 14.3 of the Services Agreement it is an operational communication only. Apex's letter of June 22, 2026 nevertheless treats that email as its notice and claims relief for the whole period from May 21, 2026 to June 10, 2026.

The flood itself would probably qualify. Clause 8.4(b) of the Services Agreement excludes delays of a supplier unless they are caused by an event that would be a Force Majeure Event if it affected Apex directly, and a flood would be such an event. The defense therefore turns on notice. We have not yet found Delaware authority on strict compliance with contractual notice conditions of this kind, and further research is needed.

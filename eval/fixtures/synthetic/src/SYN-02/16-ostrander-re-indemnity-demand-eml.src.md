Mr. Arai,

I write on behalf of Ostrander Billing Solutions LLC ("OBS") in response
to your email and letter of June 13, 2025, which demand USD 1,317,583.20
from OBS under Section 11.1(c) of the Master Services Agreement (the
"MSA"). OBS does not accept the demand, for the following reasons.

1. Notice was timely. Ms. Okafor called your client's CISO, Mr.
Wierzbicki, on March 10, 2025, the day after the event, and OBS followed
up in writing on March 14, 2025, within 48 hours of confirming on March
12 that Larkspur systems were affected. In any case, Section 22.3 of the
MSA provides that the BAA controls with respect to PHI. Section 2.4 of
the BAA gives OBS ten business days after discovery to report a breach,
which on your own dates ran to March 21, 2025. Our notice was well
within the controlling period.

2. No breach of Section 9.2. FS-BILL-03 was a transient staging server
outside the production environment described in Exhibit C. OBS's
production databases were encrypted at rest throughout, as your own
consultant's report confirms. The files on FS-BILL-03 were there because
Larkspur sends them to us every night by SFTP.

3. The general cap applies. Section 12.4 (added by the First Amendment)
sets a Data Breach Cap for "any breach of Section 9 (Data Security)".
There was no breach of Section 9, so Section 12.4 is not engaged, and
any liability of OBS is subject to the general cap in Section 12.2:
the total Fees paid or payable in the twelve months immediately
preceding the event giving rise to the claim. The event was March 9,
2025. The Fees for March 2024 through February 2025 were:

USD 748,230.00, and we do not accept that it reaches that amount.

4. Consequential loss; credit monitoring. Call center costs and credit
monitoring are consequential losses excluded by Section 12.1. Even if
they were recoverable, the industry standard for an incident of this
kind is 12 months of monitoring, and the MSA says 12 months. Larkspur's
decision to offer 24 months doubles the cost and is not reasonable. We
also do not accept that OBS should pay for Cobaltline to duplicate the
investigation Ironvale was already performing.

## 12. Correct analysis (what a careful lawyer should conclude)
- **Notice period**: nine months (Deed cl 4.1). Last day 24 September 2025. Served (if validly) 28/29 August 2025:
  in time.
- **Condition (a)**: only the Annual Rent matters (cl 9.2(a) and the clause 1.1 definition of "the Annual Rent").
  All Annual Rent to 29 September 2025 is paid (the 2023 late payment was paid, with interest). The unpaid balancing
  charge and Insurance Rent are "Rents" but not "Annual Rent", so they do not affect the break; PG's and the trainee's
  reading is wrong. They are still debts (forfeiture risk under cl 8 after 21 days; interest under cl 3.4; Schedule 4
  Part 1 para 7.2 says pay the certified sum pending dispute), so paying them, under protest, is sensible but not a
  break condition. To be safe the tenant should also pay the Annual Rent instalment due on 24 June 2026 (the Break
  Date itself) because "due up to the Break Date" is open to argument and the Lease has no refund provision.
- **Condition (b)**: vacant possession on 24 June 2026: future. **Condition (c)** (added by the Deed): Deferred Rent
  £43,250.00 paid 20 December 2021, before 24 December 2021: satisfied.
- **Name**: weak point for the landlord. A change of name does not change the company; the notice gives the correct
  company number 05928814; no reasonable recipient would be misled.
- **Address**: the stronger point, and it favours the landlord. On 28 August 2025 Carrow House was neither the
  registered office nor an address notified for service under cl 21.3 (the 3 May 2024 letter, received at the store on
  8 May 2024, notified Bristol from 1 June 2024; cl 21.5 permits the landlord to notify the tenant at the Premises).
  Under s.196(4) recorded delivery works only if addressed to the last-known place of abode or business; after the
  written notification Carrow House was no longer the last-known place of business. The emailed copy is excluded by

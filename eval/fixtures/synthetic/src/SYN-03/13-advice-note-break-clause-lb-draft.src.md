# ADVICE NOTE (DRAFT)

To: Sofia Marchetti
From: Lena Brandt, Trainee Solicitor
Date: 23 October 2025
Client / matter: Ashdown & Pryce Retail Limited - ASH.0007
Re: Unit 4, Merrow Arcade, Bath - validity of the Tenant's break notice; Pellow Grange letter of 17 October 2025

Prepared for the purpose of giving legal advice. Not to be sent to the client in this form.

## 1. Summary

1.1 In my view the break notice dated 28 August 2025 is likely to be valid. The name and address points taken by Pellow Grange ("PG") in their letter of 17 October 2025 are answerable, and their argument that the notice was out of time is wrong.

1.2 The real risk is the break conditions. Because the Lease makes the break conditional on payment of "the Rents", the unpaid service charge balancing charge and insurance rent must be paid before the Break Date or the break will fail. This is easily cured.

1.3 As a precaution we should also serve a fresh break notice on the landlord in its new name at its Bristol address before 24 December 2025.

## 2. Background

2.1 The Lease is dated 24 June 2016 and is for a term of 15 years from and including 24 June 2016. The Annual Rent is currently £94,250 a year following the 2021 rent review (memorandum dated 11 January 2022).

2.2 The landlord was Carrow Hill Estates Limited and is now Carrow Hill Property Holdings Limited (company number 05928814). The managing agent is Quarrington Lake Property Management ("QLPM").

2.3 On 28 August 2025 we sent a break notice by recorded delivery to the landlord at Carrow House, 11 Ashgrove Terrace, Bath BA2 6LP, being the landlord's registered office as stated in the Lease, and emailed a copy to Karen Pilcher at QLPM. Proof of posting is on file.

2.4 PG say (a) the notice was sent to the wrong address, (b) it was addressed to the wrong name, (c) the break conditions are not met because the client has not paid the service charge balancing charge (£7,342.18) and an insurance rent invoice (£1,906.40), and (d) the client has a history of late payment.

## 3. The break clause

3.1 Clause 9.1 of the Lease allows the Tenant to determine the Lease on 24 June 2026 (the Break Date) "by giving to the Landlord not less than six (6) months' prior written notice". The last date for service of a break notice is therefore 24 December 2025, and our notice dated 28 August 2025 was served almost four months early.

3.2 {==PG's assertion at paragraph 5 of their letter that the period for service had expired by 6 October 2025 is simply wrong.==}{>>SM: Good - this is our strongest point. Lead with it in the reply.<<} Even on PG's own case that the notice only reached the landlord on 6 October 2025, that was more than eight months before the Break Date and well within the six-month period.

3.3 Clause 9.2 provides that a break notice is only effective if on the Break Date (a) the Tenant has paid the rent due up to the Break Date and (b) the Tenant gives vacant possession of the whole of the Premises. Time is of the essence (clause 9.4).

## 4. The break conditions

4.1 Clause 1.1 defines "the Rents" as the Annual Rent, the Service Charge, the Insurance Rent and all other sums reserved as rent. As PG point out, condition (a) is therefore not limited to the principal rent: every sum reserved as rent and due up to the Break Date must have been paid.

4.2 The client's ledger shows that all quarterly rent has been paid. However, the following are unpaid:

(a) service charge balancing charge for the year ended 24 March 2025: £7,342.18 (certificate dated 30 June 2025, due within 21 days);

(b) insurance rent (terrorism cover): £1,906.40 (demanded 18 August 2025, due within 14 days);

total £9,248.58. Interest at the Default Rate under clause 3.4 is also reserved as rent and should be paid with them. We should ask QLPM for the interest figure.

4.3 Daniel Okafor is querying the atrium roof works that account for most of the balancing charge. That query does not justify withholding payment: paragraph 7.2 of Part 1 of Schedule 4 requires the Tenant to pay the certified sum pending resolution of any dispute. I recommend that the client pays £9,248.58 plus interest now, under protest and expressly without prejudice to its query, so that condition (a) is satisfied well before the Break Date.

4.4 The late payment of the June 2023 quarter does not matter for the break: the condition is tested on the Break Date, and the late payment was made good (with interest) in 2023.

4.5 Vacant possession: the client must remove its stock, staff and loose fixtures and return the keys on the Break Date. Whether the mezzanine and shopfront must come out depends on whether the landlord serves a reinstatement notice under the Licence for Alterations.

## 5. Service

5.1 Clause 21.2 applies section 196 of the Law of Property Act 1925. Under section 196(4) a notice sent by recorded delivery is sufficiently served if it is addressed to the landlord at its last-known place of abode or business and is not returned undelivered. Our letter was not returned.

5.2 PG rely on the landlord's letter of 3 May 2024 notifying a new address in Bristol from 1 June 2024. That letter was addressed to the store rather than to the client's registered office, and Daniel Okafor has no record of it reaching the property team. It is arguable that the new address was not "notified to the Tenant" for the purposes of clause 21.3.

5.3 Name: the notice gives the landlord's company number. A change of name does not create a new company, and no reasonable recipient could have been in doubt as to which company the notice was meant for. I think this point is weak.

5.4 The emailed copy to QLPM was sent for information only and we should not rely on it as service (clause 21.4).

5.5 If a court were to find that Carrow House was not a permitted address on 28 August 2025, the notice would not have been validly served. That is the main residual risk on service and is the reason for the fresh notice recommended below.

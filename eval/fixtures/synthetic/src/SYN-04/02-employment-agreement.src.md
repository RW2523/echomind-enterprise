# EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT

This EMPLOYMENT AGREEMENT (this "Agreement") is entered into as of August 18, 2021 (the "Effective Date") by and between Brackenridge Analytics, Inc., a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business at 77 Almaden Crossing, San Jose, California 95113 (the "Company"), and Daniela Okafor-Reyes, an individual residing at 2290 Larkmead Court, Campbell, California 95008 ("Employee").

RECITALS

A. The Company has offered Employee employment as Senior Data Scientist on the terms of an offer letter dated August 16, 2021 (the "Offer Letter").

B. The Company and Employee wish to set out the terms and conditions of Employee's employment, Employee's obligations with respect to the Company's confidential information and inventions, and the manner in which disputes between them will be resolved.

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of Employee's employment, the compensation and benefits described below, and the mutual promises in this Agreement, the parties agree as follows.

1. POSITION AND DUTIES

(a) Employee will be employed in the position of Senior Data Scientist, reporting to the Company's Director of Data Science or such other person as the Company may designate. Employee will perform the duties customarily associated with that position and such other duties consistent with Employee's skills as the Company may assign from time to time.

(b) Employee will devote Employee's full business time, attention and skill to the Company's business and will comply with the Company's policies as in effect from time to time.

(c) Employee's principal place of work will be the Company's headquarters in San Jose, California, subject to the Company's remote and hybrid work policy.

2. START DATE

Employee's employment will begin on September 7, 2021 (the "Start Date"). Employee's employment will continue until terminated in accordance with Section 7.

3. COMPENSATION

(a) Base Salary. The Company will pay Employee a base salary at the annualized rate of One Hundred Sixty-Eight Thousand Dollars ($168,000.00) ("Base Salary"), less applicable withholdings and deductions, payable in accordance with the Company's regular payroll practices (currently biweekly). Base Salary will be reviewed periodically and may be adjusted by the Company in its discretion. Employee is classified as exempt from overtime.

(b) Annual Bonus. Employee will be eligible to participate in the Company's annual bonus plan as in effect from time to time, with a target bonus of fifteen percent (15%) of Base Salary earned during the applicable plan year. The amount of any bonus will be determined in accordance with the terms of the applicable plan, including Company performance and Employee's individual performance. Any bonus is subject to the terms and conditions of the applicable plan, including any requirement that Employee remain employed through the payment date, and nothing in this Section 3(b) guarantees any bonus.

(c) Sign-On Bonus. The Company will pay Employee a one-time sign-on bonus of Fifteen Thousand Dollars ($15,000.00), less applicable withholdings, with the first regular payroll following Employee's completion of thirty (30) days of employment. If, within twelve (12) months after the Start Date, Employee resigns or the Company terminates Employee's employment for Cause, Employee will repay a pro rata portion of the gross sign-on bonus equal to the number of full months remaining in that twelve-month period divided by twelve. "Cause" means (i) Employee's willful misconduct or gross negligence in the performance of Employee's duties, (ii) Employee's conviction of, or plea of no contest to, a felony or a crime involving dishonesty, (iii) Employee's material breach of Section 4, or (iv) Employee's material violation of a written Company policy prohibiting harassment, discrimination or retaliation, in each case as reasonably determined by the Company.

(d) Equity. Subject to approval by the Company's Board of Directors, Employee will be granted an option to purchase 24,000 shares of the Company's common stock under the Brackenridge Analytics, Inc. 2019 Equity Incentive Plan (the "Plan"), with an exercise price equal to the fair market value on the date of grant and a vesting commencement date equal to the Start Date. The option will be governed exclusively by the Plan, the stock option grant notice and the stock option agreement entered into under the Plan (together, the "Equity Documents"), which will control in the event of any conflict with this Agreement.

4. CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION AND INVENTIONS

(a) Confidential Information. "Confidential Information" means all non-public information, in any form, that Employee learns or develops in the course of employment and that relates to the business, products, technology, customers, finances or personnel of the Company or its customers, including source code, models, training data, customer datasets, product roadmaps, pricing, customer lists and financial results. Confidential Information does not include information that (i) is or becomes generally available to the public other than through Employee's breach, (ii) Employee can show was lawfully known to Employee before the Start Date, or (iii) Employee lawfully receives from a third party without a duty of confidentiality. Confidential Information also does not include information about Employee's own wages, hours or other terms and conditions of employment.

(b) Non-Use and Non-Disclosure. During and after employment, Employee will hold all Confidential Information in strict confidence, will not disclose it to anyone outside the Company except as required in the performance of Employee's duties or as permitted by Section 4(e), and will not use it for any purpose other than the Company's business.

(c) Inventions.

(i) Employee agrees that all inventions, discoveries, works of authorship, models, software, data compilations, improvements and know-how that Employee makes, conceives or reduces to practice, alone or with others, during the period of employment and that relate to the Company's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development, or that result from work performed by Employee for the Company ("Company Inventions"), are the sole property of the Company. Employee hereby assigns to the Company all right, title and interest in and to the Company Inventions and will sign any documents reasonably requested by the Company to perfect that assignment, during and after employment.

(ii) This Section 4(c) does not apply to any invention that qualifies fully for protection under California Labor Code Section 2870, that is, an invention that Employee developed entirely on Employee's own time without using the Company's equipment, supplies, facilities or trade secret information, except for inventions that either relate at the time of conception or reduction to practice to the Company's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development, or result from any work performed by Employee for the Company. The written notice required by California Labor Code Section 2872 is attached as Exhibit B.

(iii) Employee has listed on Exhibit A all inventions that Employee made before the Start Date and wishes to exclude from this Agreement ("Prior Inventions"). If Exhibit A is blank or states "None," Employee represents that there are no Prior Inventions.

(d) Return of Property. Upon termination of employment, or earlier on request, Employee will promptly return to the Company all Company property, including laptops, badges, devices, documents and copies of Confidential Information in any form, and will permanently delete any Confidential Information from personal devices and accounts after first returning a copy to the Company.

(e) Protected Activity. Nothing in this Agreement prohibits or restricts Employee from (i) filing a charge or complaint with, communicating with, providing information to, or participating in an investigation by the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, the California Civil Rights Department, the National Labor Relations Board, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the California Labor Commissioner or any other federal, state or local agency; (ii) discussing or disclosing information about unlawful acts in the workplace, such as harassment or discrimination or any other conduct that Employee has reason to believe is unlawful, consistent with California Government Code Section 12964.5; (iii) discussing wages, hours or other terms and conditions of employment; or (iv) testifying truthfully in any legal proceeding. Pursuant to the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), Employee will not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret that is made in confidence to a government official or to an attorney solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law, or that is made in a complaint or other document filed under seal in a lawsuit or other proceeding.

5. NON-SOLICITATION OF EMPLOYEES

(a) During Employee's employment and for a period of twelve (12) months after the termination of Employee's employment for any reason, Employee will not, directly or indirectly, solicit, induce or encourage any employee of the Company with whom Employee worked or about whom Employee obtained Confidential Information to terminate his or her employment with the Company in order to work for any other person or entity. General advertisements not targeted at Company employees do not violate this Section 5(a).

(b) Nothing in this Agreement is intended to, or shall be construed to, prevent Employee from engaging in any lawful profession, trade or business after the termination of employment. If any provision of this Section 5 would be unenforceable under California Business and Professions Code Section 16600 or other applicable law, that provision shall be severed or limited to the minimum extent necessary, and the remainder of this Agreement shall remain in effect.

(c) The Company does not require, and this Agreement does not contain, any covenant by Employee not to compete with the Company after the termination of employment.

6. OUTSIDE ACTIVITIES

During employment, Employee will not engage in any outside employment, consulting or business activity that competes with the Company, that interferes with the performance of Employee's duties, or that uses Company resources or Confidential Information, without the prior written consent of the Company's General Counsel.

7. AT-WILL EMPLOYMENT

Employee's employment with the Company is and will remain "at will." Either Employee or the Company may terminate the employment relationship at any time, with or without Cause and with or without advance notice. The at-will nature of Employee's employment may be modified only by a written agreement expressly so providing and signed by Employee and the Company's Chief Executive Officer. No Company handbook, policy, practice, performance improvement plan, progressive discipline procedure or oral statement alters the at-will nature of Employee's employment or creates any right to employment for any period of time or to any particular procedure before termination.

8. BENEFITS AND PAID TIME OFF

Employee will be eligible to participate in the employee benefit plans and programs the Company generally makes available to similarly situated employees, subject to the terms of those plans, which may be amended or terminated by the Company at any time. Employee will accrue paid time off in accordance with the Company's paid time off policy as in effect from time to time. Accrued and unused paid time off will be paid to Employee upon termination of employment as required by California Labor Code Section 227.3.

9. ARBITRATION OF DISPUTES

(a) Agreement to Arbitrate. To the fullest extent permitted by law, the Company and Employee agree that any and all disputes, claims or controversies arising out of or relating to this Agreement, Employee's employment with the Company, the termination of that employment, or Employee's compensation or benefits, whether based on contract, tort, statute or any other legal theory, and including claims for wages, penalties, discrimination, harassment or retaliation under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act, the California Fair Employment and Housing Act, the California Labor Code (including Section 1102.5) and any other federal, state or local law (collectively, "Covered Claims"), shall be resolved exclusively by final and binding arbitration as provided in this Section 9. This agreement to arbitrate is mutual and binds the Company to the same extent as Employee.

(b) Arbitrator and Location. Arbitration shall be conducted before a single neutral arbitrator who is a retired state or federal judge or an attorney with at least ten (10) years of experience in employment law. The parties shall select the arbitrator by mutual agreement within thirty (30) days after a written demand for arbitration is served. If they cannot agree, the arbitrator shall be selected by alternately striking names from a list of seven (7) qualified neutrals provided by a neutral arbitration service agreed by the parties, or, failing such agreement, appointed by the Superior Court of California, County of Santa Clara, under California Code of Civil Procedure Section 1281.6. The arbitration hearing shall take place in Santa Clara County, California, unless the parties agree otherwise in writing.

(c) Fees and Costs. The Company shall pay all fees and costs unique to arbitration, including the arbitrator's compensation, any arbitration filing or administrative fees, and the cost of the hearing room, and shall pay them within the time required by California Code of Civil Procedure Sections 1281.97 and 1281.98. If Employee initiates arbitration, Employee shall not be required to pay more than the amount Employee would have paid to file a complaint in the Superior Court of California. Each party shall bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, except that the arbitrator shall award attorneys' fees and costs to the prevailing party to the extent a court could do so under applicable law.

(d) Procedure. The arbitrator shall apply the substantive law of California and applicable federal law, and may award any remedy that would be available in a court of competent jurisdiction. The discovery provisions of the California Arbitration Act, California Code of Civil Procedure Section 1280 et seq., shall apply, and the parties shall be entitled to adequate discovery. The arbitrator shall issue a written, reasoned award setting out the essential findings and conclusions on which the award is based. Judgment on the award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.

(e) Claims Not Covered. This Section 9 does not apply to:

(i) claims for workers' compensation benefits;

(ii) claims for unemployment insurance or state disability insurance benefits;

(iii) claims for benefits under an employee benefit plan governed by the Employee Retirement Income Security Act that specifies a different claims procedure;

(iv) the filing of an administrative charge or complaint with any government agency, including those identified in Section 4(e), although any claim for individual monetary relief that is not resolved by the agency and that is a Covered Claim shall be arbitrated;

(v) representative claims under the California Private Attorneys General Act to the extent such claims cannot lawfully be required to be arbitrated;

(vi) applications by either party to a court for provisional remedies in aid of arbitration, including a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction to prevent a breach of Section 4, as permitted by California Code of Civil Procedure Section 1281.8; and

(vii) any dispute relating to a sexual harassment dispute or sexual assault dispute that Employee elects to bring in court, as provided in 9 U.S.C. §§ 401–402.

(f) Individual Claims. To the fullest extent permitted by law, Covered Claims shall be brought and arbitrated on an individual basis only, and not on a class or collective basis. The arbitrator shall have no authority to consolidate claims of different employees or to preside over any form of class or collective proceeding. Any dispute about the enforceability of this Section 9(f) shall be decided by a court, not the arbitrator.

(g) Governing Law of Arbitration. This Section 9 is governed by the Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. § 1 et seq., and, to the extent not inconsistent with it, the California Arbitration Act. If any part of this Section 9 is found unenforceable, that part shall be severed and the remainder enforced, except as provided in Section 9(f).

(h) Acknowledgment. EMPLOYEE ACKNOWLEDGES THAT EMPLOYEE HAS CAREFULLY READ THIS SECTION 9, UNDERSTANDS ITS TERMS, HAS HAD THE OPPORTUNITY TO CONSULT WITH AN ATTORNEY OF EMPLOYEE'S CHOICE, AND IS KNOWINGLY AND VOLUNTARILY WAIVING THE RIGHT TO A JURY OR COURT TRIAL OF COVERED CLAIMS.

10. EXPENSE REIMBURSEMENT

The Company will reimburse Employee for all necessary expenditures or losses incurred by Employee in direct consequence of the discharge of Employee's duties, as required by California Labor Code Section 2802, in accordance with the Company's expense reimbursement policy as in effect from time to time, including any policy on reimbursement of home internet and mobile telephone expenses for remote and hybrid employees.

11. TAXES; SECTION 409A

All payments under this Agreement are subject to applicable tax withholdings and deductions. The parties intend that all payments under this Agreement be exempt from or comply with Section 409A of the Internal Revenue Code and the regulations under it, and this Agreement shall be interpreted accordingly.

12. ENTIRE AGREEMENT

This Agreement, together with the Offer Letter and the Equity Documents, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties with respect to its subject matter and supersedes all prior negotiations, representations and agreements, written or oral. In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and the Offer Letter, this Agreement controls. The Company's Employee Handbook and its other personnel policies, as in effect from time to time, are not part of this Agreement, do not create any contractual rights, and may be adopted, amended or withdrawn by the Company at any time in its discretion; in the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any such handbook or policy, this Agreement controls. This Agreement may be amended only by a written instrument signed by Employee and the Company's Chief Executive Officer.

13. GOVERNING LAW

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of California, without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles. Subject to Section 9, any action that is not subject to arbitration shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in Santa Clara County, California, and the parties consent to the personal jurisdiction of those courts.

14. SEVERABILITY

If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect, and the invalid or unenforceable provision shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable, subject to Sections 5(b) and 9(g).

15. NOTICES

Notices under this Agreement shall be in writing and delivered by hand, by overnight courier or by email, to the Company at its headquarters address, attention: General Counsel, and to Employee at the most recent home address and personal email address on file with the Company.

16. ASSIGNMENT; SUCCESSORS

This Agreement is personal to Employee and may not be assigned by Employee. The Company may assign this Agreement to any successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets.

17. COUNTERPARTS; ELECTRONIC SIGNATURES

This Agreement may be signed in counterparts, each of which is an original and all of which together are one instrument. Signatures delivered by electronic means, including PDF or an electronic signature service, are effective as originals.

## EXHIBIT B - NOTICE UNDER CALIFORNIA LABOR CODE SECTION 2872

In accordance with California Labor Code Section 2872, Employee is notified that Section 4(c) of this Agreement does not apply to an invention that qualifies fully under California Labor Code Section 2870. Section 2870 provides, in summary, that a provision in an employment agreement requiring an employee to assign to the employer rights in an invention does not apply to an invention that the employee developed entirely on his or her own time without using the employer's equipment, supplies, facilities or trade secret information, except for those inventions that either (1) relate at the time of conception or reduction to practice of the invention to the employer's business, or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development of the employer, or (2) result from any work performed by the employee for the employer. Any provision purporting to require assignment of such an invention is unenforceable.
